---

Although it certainly WAS sin, taking multiple wives/concubines was not viewed as adulterous in the
culture of that day. Even so, Deuteronomy tells us that one of the standards for Hebrew kings was to
refrain from doing just that. David, who was incredibly commendable in several respects, completely and
willingly failed with this command. In doing so, a progressive desensitization to sin and consequent
inner descent from holiness had taken hold in his life. In other words, this minimized sin choked out
God's holy call on his life AND to the awareness to the danger thereof, priming him for the grand fall
with Bathesheba and the devastating consequences that followed.

What are examples of "legal" sensualities in our society? Pornography, graphic movies/shows/novels,
hyper-sexualized media/marketing, crude humor, hookup culture... the list goes on. However, the root of
this is moral relaxation. All of the things mentioned prior in 2 Samuel are a result of a lapse in
discipline in David's physical life (not going to war and being idle) that extended to his spirituality.
As believers, we must continue to train to toil, strive, and train for godliness as Paul advises in
1 Timothy 4... work up a spiritual sweat to avoid wilting under the heat of sensuality.
